# Preliminary Care Guideline for Common Conditions (Mock Data for Learning)

This document is mock data for learning purposes, used only to demonstrate a RAG pipeline. It is not a substitute for a doctor's diagnosis.

## Common Cold

Common symptoms: nasal congestion, runny nose, sneezing, sore throat, mild cough, possibly a low-grade fever (usually no higher than 38.5°C); overall symptoms are mild.
Possible causes: upper respiratory viral infection such as rhinovirus or coronavirus, often after getting chilled or overtired.
Initial advice: rest and drink plenty of fluids; if fever or sore throat is significant, symptomatic medicine such as acetaminophen may be used under a pharmacist's guidance. The common cold is caused by a virus and usually does not need antibiotics. It typically lasts 5–7 days.
Recommended department: Respiratory Medicine or General Practice.
Seek medical care promptly if: high fever lasts more than 3 days, difficulty breathing, chest pain, symptoms last more than 10 days, or symptoms get worse after improving.

## Influenza

Common symptoms: sudden high fever (up to 39–40°C), chills, headache, body aches, marked fatigue, possibly with sore throat and dry cough. Overall symptoms are more severe than the common cold.
Possible causes: influenza A or B virus infection; contagious, most common in winter and spring.
Initial advice: stay home and rest to avoid infecting others; drink plenty of fluids; seeing a doctor within 48 hours of onset allows assessment for antivirals such as oseltamivir.
Recommended department: Fever Clinic or Respiratory Medicine.
Seek medical care promptly if: the patient is elderly, a child, pregnant, or has a chronic illness; or there is rapid breathing, persistent high fever, altered consciousness, or chest pain.

## Acute Bronchitis

Common symptoms: mainly cough, dry at first and later with white or yellow phlegm, possibly with low-grade fever and discomfort behind the breastbone; the cough can last 1–3 weeks.
Possible causes: mostly viral infection, often following a cold.
Initial advice: drink plenty of fluids, keep indoor air humid, avoid smoking and irritating fumes; if coughing disrupts sleep, cough and expectorant medicine may be used under a doctor's guidance.
Recommended department: Respiratory Medicine.
Seek medical care promptly if: cough lasts more than 3 weeks, coughing up blood, persistent high fever, wheezing or difficulty breathing.

## Migraine

Common symptoms: throbbing headache on one or both sides, moderate to severe, worse with activity, often with nausea, vomiting, and sensitivity to light and sound; each attack lasts 4–72 hours.
Possible triggers: lack of sleep, stress, alcohol, menstruation, certain foods, etc.
Initial advice: rest in a quiet, dark room during an attack; keep a headache diary to identify triggers; maintain a regular routine.
Recommended department: Neurology.
Seek medical care promptly if: sudden severe headache (the worst headache of your life), headache with fever and neck stiffness, headache with limb weakness or slurred speech, or a clear change in headache pattern.

## Acute Gastroenteritis

Common symptoms: abdominal pain, diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, possibly fever, usually starting a few hours to 1 day after eating contaminated food.
Possible causes: bacterial or viral infection, or food poisoning.
Initial advice: eat bland food, drink small amounts of fluid frequently, and use oral rehydration salts to prevent dehydration; with severe vomiting and diarrhea, stop eating for a few hours.
Recommended department: Gastroenterology or Emergency.
Seek medical care promptly if: signs of dehydration (dry mouth, reduced urination, dizziness), blood in stool, high fever, or severe or worsening abdominal pain.

## Hypertension

Common symptoms: most patients have no obvious symptoms; some have dizziness, headache, neck tightness, or palpitations.
Diagnostic criteria: on 3 measurements taken on different days, systolic pressure ≥ 140 mmHg and/or diastolic pressure ≥ 90 mmHg.
Initial advice: low-salt diet (less than 5 g of salt per day), weight control, regular exercise, quit smoking and limit alcohol; patients already on blood pressure medication should take it regularly as prescribed and not stop on their own.
Recommended department: Cardiology.
Seek medical care promptly if: blood pressure exceeds 180/110 mmHg, or with severe headache, chest pain, blurred vision, or limb weakness.

## Lumbar Muscle Strain

Common symptoms: aching or dull pain in the lower back, worse after exertion, better with rest, noticeably uncomfortable after bending for a long time.
Possible causes: prolonged sitting, poor posture, repeated minor injury to the lower back muscles.
Initial advice: avoid sitting or standing for long periods and mind your posture; apply local heat; strengthen the lower back muscles; if pain is significant, topical pain relief may be used under a doctor's guidance.
Recommended department: Orthopedics or Rehabilitation Medicine.
Seek medical care promptly if: back pain with numbness, radiating pain or weakness in the legs, or abnormal bladder or bowel function.
